Our Core Proposals

5 Actionable Milestones for Healthy Learning

Here is what we are asking the Hopewell Valley Board of Education and administration to adopt into policy:

01

Analog-First Learning

Screens down, pencils up!

The evidence is clear: reading physical books and writing by hand increases student comprehension, retention, mental focus, and academic performance. Devices, in contrast, have been shown to encourage distraction, multitasking, cognitive fatigue, and anxiety, while disrupting social-emotional learning, relational connection, and sleep.

Policy Goal: Embrace analog tools as the default across subjects, unless a learning objective specifically requires a digital device.
02

Measure School-Device Screen Time

Data Transparency

How many hours per day are students currently spending looking at school-issued screens? Right now, we don’t actually know. To get a complete picture of school-related screen use, we propose following in the footsteps of the Los Angeles Unified School District (LAUSD)—tracking and measuring all student time spent on school-issued devices.

Policy Goal: Track and audit school-device screentime, and make transparent aggregate usage data easily accessible to parents.
03

Daily Limits on Classroom Screen Time

Key Recommendation

As districts like LA, NYC, and others have done, we propose establishing clear, enforceable daily time limits for student screen time (excluding courses where the subject matter itself is inherently digital, such as computer science, robotics programming, or typing class, and allowing for extraordinary case-by-case exceptions).

Primary
PreK – 2nd Grade
0 Screen Time
Screen-free classroom foundation; tactile & social learning
Elementary
Grades 3rd – 5th
≤ 30 mins / day
Focused, brief research or math checks; physical books default
Middle School
Grades 6th – 8th
≤ 45 mins / day
Timberlane Middle; protecting peer engagement & study focus
High School
Grades 9th – 12th
≤ 45 mins / day*
*With relevant exceptions for CS, robotics, audio/digital arts
04

End Routine Take-Home Chromebooks

Protecting Family Time

In an analog-first ecosystem, homework should be analog by default. Chromebooks should stay at school unless a family requests otherwise, or an exceptional project requires it.

“Currently, mandatory take-home Chromebooks can function as an educational Trojan horse, bringing open-ended digital distraction into our homes under the mantle of required schoolwork. By mandating screens within the home, the district unintentionally undermines parental authority and displaces essential family time, unstructured time, outdoor time, and the option of screen-free evenings and weekends.”
Policy Goal: Devices remain in charging carts at school by default. Homework returns to paper, books, and conversation.
05

Form a Working Group Within 60 Days

Prompt Implementation

To help turn these goals into formal district policy, we recommend forming a working group comprised of parents, educators, administrators, and outside experts (where available). This group would co-create a detailed pathway forward and keep Hopewell Valley striding toward our milestones in a timely manner.

Policy Goal: Convene the collaborative task force this fall with regular reporting to the Board of Ed.

Important Protection for Accommodations

Please note that these goals, milestones, and limits would not apply to students with an IEP or Section 504 Plan whose accommodations require assistive technology or screen-based tools. We champion full support and access for every child who relies on specialized digital assistive devices.

  Hey YEW!


  "How are you?" is often treated as a polite reflex—a routine greeting or an easy way to fill a silence. At ecoPTO, we ask this question with genuine intention: *How are our Youth? How is our Earth? How is our community’s Wellbeing?*


  At the center of our YEW initiative is a simple, shared purpose: energizing and expanding our connection to the natural world. While the mission is easy to champion, the daily reality our families and schools face tells a different story. In his 2005 book *Last Child in the Woods*, Richard Louv coined the term "Nature-Deficit Disorder." While not a medical diagnosis, it offers vital language for an undeniable cultural reality. Our days are increasingly lived indoors, defined by screen time, rigid routines, and structured obligations. This is neither a personal shortcoming nor an institutional critique; it is simply the default pattern of modern life.


  What happens if we intentionally shift that pattern together?


  When we integrate nature back into the rhythm of daily life—both in the classroom and at home—the benefits ripple across our entire community:


  - **Curiosity and learning expand**, as outdoor exploration naturally sparks engagement and problem-solving.

  - **Mental and physical health improve**, offering students, educators, and parents a meaningful antidote to daily stress.

  - **Community bonds strengthen**, rooted in shared stewardship of the environment we call home.


  There is already so much thoughtful, dedicated work happening across our district and in our households. ecoPTO is grateful to partner with both our families and our educational leaders as we explore small, intentional ways to step outside and reconnect with what sustains us all.\

  Kristin, writing on behalf of ecoPTO
